Vendor Check

Free supplier checks for people starting a lash or hair-extension business. Each check below takes a few minutes, and each claim links to its source. Nothing here is a recommendation of any supplier, and we have not ordered from any of them.

1. "Gold Supplier" is a paid membership, not an audit

A Gold Supplier badge means the seller pays for an Alibaba membership. Sourcing guides describe the check behind it as basic business-licence verification, not a visit to the factory. Treat it as "this seller pays to be here", nothing more.

Sources: Cosmo Sourcing, Cosmo Sourcing, "Is Alibaba legit?"

2. "Verified Supplier" comes with a report. Read it

Alibaba says a Verified Supplier has passed a third-party inspection covering its business licence, facilities, quality control and R&D, and that a capability report is published on the storefront. The audit is a point-in-time check of the company and does not test the products it makes. Open that report and check that the factory address and product lines match what you are buying (lash trays, not general cosmetics).

Source: Alibaba.com seller site, Verified Supplier

3. Trade Assurance only protects money paid on Alibaba

Alibaba's Trade Assurance covers payment, delivery and product quality on orders you place and pay for on Alibaba.com. If a seller asks you to pay by bank transfer, PayPal or WhatsApp invoice outside the platform, that order has no Trade Assurance. Check the "Protection with Trade Assurance" box on the product page before you pay.

Sources: Alibaba.com, "Stay on platform, stay protected", Guided Imports

4. Compare years on Alibaba with the revenue it claims

A storefront shows how many years it has been on Alibaba and a revenue band. One lash storefront we checked showed 1 year on the platform next to a $10M–$50M revenue band. That is not proof of anything, but it is a question to ask before you order: "Which factory makes your trays, and since when?"

Source: our lash vendor directory, checked 2026-09-19 against the live storefront.

5. Look up the age of the seller's own website

If the supplier has its own domain, look it up on ICANN Lookup and read the creation date. A website older than the Alibaba storefront is a mild sign the business existed before it started selling there. A domain registered last month for a "10-year factory" is worth a question.

Source: ICANN Registration Data Lookup

6. "No scam reports found" means nothing was found

Search the company's exact registered name with words like "scam", "complaint" and "review". If nothing comes up, that is all it means: a new or small seller can simply have no reviews yet. Watch for name collisions: a complaint about "Love Hair" is not a complaint about "Lody Love Hair".

7. Before the sample order: a checklist

  • Order the exact style you plan to sell (curl, length, band), not the seller's best sample.
  • Pay on the platform, so the sample has Trade Assurance (check 3).
  • Ask for the MOQ, unit price at that MOQ, and lead time in writing in the chat.
  • When it arrives, photograph it next to the listing photos before you use it.
  • Ask whether your bulk order ships from the same factory as the sample.

8. Factory or trading company? Read the business scope

Many sellers on Alibaba call themselves factories. Ask for the business licence: a factory's business scope must include words like "manufacture" or "produce", and a trading company's will not. A factory usually specialises in one or a few kinds of products, so a storefront selling lashes, phone cases and wigs side by side points to a trader. A trading company is not a bad sign in itself. What matters is that you know which one you are buying from.

Source: Jingsourcing, "7 ways to verify Chinese suppliers are factories, not trading companies", checked 2026-10-14.

9. MOQ is the smallest order they accept, and it can move

Minimum order quantity (MOQ) is the lowest number of units a supplier lets you buy at one time. Alibaba's own guide says that in some cases you can negotiate it with the seller, and that a buyer who wants only a few units will likely pay the retail price rather than the wholesale price. Ask for the unit price at the quantity you will actually order, not the price shown for the top tier.

Source: Alibaba.com business blog, "What is minimum order quantity (MOQ)?", checked 2026-10-14.

10. What a sample should cost

For ready-made products the sample fee is usually the product cost plus shipping, and some quoted fees leave shipping out, so ask what is included. On a first order a supplier will generally not give free samples. Some suppliers refund the sample fee when you place a bulk order. If yours offers that, get it in writing in the chat before you pay.

Source: Jingsourcing, "Top 5 Alibaba sample questions", checked 2026-10-14.

11. EXW, FOB or DDP: what the quoted price includes

Quotes name an Incoterms rule, and it decides who pays for what. EXW (Ex Works): the seller only makes the goods available at its premises; it does not load them or clear them for export. FOB (Free on Board): the seller delivers the goods on board the vessel at the port of shipment, and the risk and all costs pass to you from that point. DDP (Delivered Duty Paid): the seller bears the costs and risks of bringing the goods to you, clears them for export and import, and pays the duties. Compare two suppliers only on the same rule.

Source: GOV.UK, "Customs valuation: Incoterms", checked 2026-10-14. The rules are published by the International Chamber of Commerce.

12. Trade Assurance has a claim window. Open the box on arrival

Alibaba's 2023 Trade Assurance guide says that if an order is not shipped, goes missing, or arrives defective, incorrect or damaged, you can claim a refund within 30 days of the delivery date (60 days for Enterprise and Enterprise Pro buyers). It also says you can cancel within 2 hours of payment, if the order has not shipped, for a full refund. Count and check the shipment the day it arrives, and check the current terms on your order page, since the guide is from 2023.

Source: Alibaba.com, Trade Assurance Guide 2023 (PDF), checked 2026-10-14.

13. Search the product photos

Save a listing photo and search it on Google (Search by image, then Upload a file). The results can include websites with the same or a similar image. If the same photo is on many other storefronts, it tells you nothing about who made the product. Ask the seller for a photo or video of the exact item you are ordering, next to a note with the seller's name and today's date.

Source: Google Search Help, "Search with an image", checked 2026-10-14.

14. PayPal to a supplier: what Purchase Protection leaves out

PayPal's US help page lists payments that are not eligible for its Purchase Protection. The list includes "Friends and Family payments" and "Items intended for resale, including single item transactions or transactions that include multiple items". Stock you buy to resell in your lash or hair business is bought for resale. So do not count on PayPal to get your money back from a supplier. On Alibaba, pay on the platform so the order has Trade Assurance (check 2).

Source: PayPal US Help Center, "Does PayPal cover my purchase if there's a problem?", checked 2026-10-19.

15. "Our bank account changed": verify on a second channel

A message saying a supplier's bank details have changed is the classic business email compromise pattern. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center advises using secondary channels or two-factor authentication to verify requests for changes in account information. Confirm the change with the supplier on a contact you already had (the Alibaba chat, or a phone number from before), not on one given in the message that asks for the change.

Source: FBI IC3 public service announcement, "Business Email Compromise: The $50 Billion Scam", 2023-06-09, checked 2026-10-19.

16. In the US, lashes and lash glue are cosmetics

The FDA says it "considers false eyelashes, eyelash extensions, and their adhesives to be cosmetic products", and that they must meet the safety and labeling requirements for cosmetics. It also advises checking the ingredients before using these adhesives. Before you put your brand on a supplier's lash glue, ask the supplier for its full ingredient list.

Source: FDA, "Eye Cosmetic Safety", checked 2026-10-19.

17. Your brand on the label: MoCRA's responsible person

Under the US Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA), the responsible person is the manufacturer, packer, or distributor whose name appears on the cosmetic's label. The responsible person must report serious adverse events to the FDA within 15 business days. MoCRA exempts certain small businesses from GMP, registration and product listing, but the FDA says those exemptions do not apply to manufacturers or facilities making products that regularly come into contact with the eye's mucous membrane, or products intended to alter appearance for more than 24 hours. Read the FDA page before you rely on the exemption.

Source: FDA, "Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA)" (content current as of 2026-08-10), checked 2026-10-19.

18. What a US cosmetic label must show: ingredients and who sells it

The FDA's labeling summary says cosmetics sold to consumers for personal care must carry an ingredient declaration, in descending order of predominance. Color additives and ingredients at one percent or less may be listed in any order. The FDA's labeling regulations page adds that the label must show the name and place of business of the manufacturer, packer or distributor, and that if the name is not the manufacturer's, the label must say "Manufactured for..." or "Distributed by...". Ask your supplier whether the ingredient list can be supplied for any lash adhesive you plan to sell under your brand.

Sources: FDA, "Summary of Cosmetics Labeling Requirements" (published 2025-11-18); FDA, "Cosmetics Labeling Regulations"; both checked 2026-10-21.

19. "Lash growth" on a supplier's listing: the FDA treats body claims as drug claims

The FDA's cosmetics labeling claims page says the law does not require cosmetic labeling to have FDA approval before products go on the market, and that the FDA does not have a list of approved or accepted claims for cosmetics. It also says that if a product's claims are about the body, it is a drug according to the law and must meet the requirements for drugs, even if it affects the appearance. Treating or preventing disease, or affecting the structure or function of the body, are the page's examples. The page does not mention lash products by name. Before you copy a supplier's product claims onto your own listing, check them against this page.

Source: FDA, "Cosmetics Labeling Claims", checked 2026-10-21.

20. Eyelash tints and dyes from a supplier: the FDA's eye-area color rules

The FDA's eye cosmetic safety page says a number of color additives approved for cosmetics in general are not approved for use in the area of the eye. It also says permanent eyelash and eyebrow tints and dyes have been known to cause serious eye injuries, including blindness. The FDA has provided for silver nitrate as a color additive to color eyebrows and eyelashes, in professional-use-only cosmetics, up to 4 percent by weight in a viscous gel, and says those products are not for distribution or direct sale to consumers. If a supplier lists a lash tint or dye for resale, ask which color additive it contains and check it against this page before you stock it.

Source: FDA, "Eye Cosmetic Safety" (page dated 2022-06-28), checked 2026-10-22.
